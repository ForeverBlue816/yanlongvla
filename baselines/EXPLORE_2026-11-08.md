  | FP | 75 | 36 | 46.3 | |
  | mxbb (2-bit backbone, FP head) | 104 | 74 | 74.2 | +27.9 [+18.7, +37.9] |
  | mxhd (FP backbone, 2-bit head) | 99 | 68 | 69.6 | +23.3 [+12.9, +33.3] |
  | QuIP# (both) | 107 | 69 | 73.3 | +27.1 [+16.7, +37.9] |
  | QTIP / VPTQ (both, other methods) | 93 / 97 | 72 / 81 | 68.8 / 74.2 | +22.5 / +27.9 |

- Episodes gained over FP (row wins, FP fails), overlap with QuIP#'s gained episodes (Jaccard): carrot mxbb 0.80, mxhd 0.67 (QTIP 0.82, VPTQ 0.77); stack_cube mxbb 0.55, mxhd 0.52 (QTIP 0.50, VPTQ 0.56). mxbb against mxhd: 0.67 carrot, 0.46 stack_cube.
- **Reading (exploratory, no claim):** the gain is not in one component. The 2-bit error in the backbone alone, or in the action head alone, gives almost the full gain, and the effects do not add (both halves together = one half). The gained episodes are mostly the same episodes for every 2-bit change (two halves, three methods). So the picture is: FP fails on a fixed set of seeded episodes, and many different 2-bit errors move the policy out of that failure, while isotropic noise of a similar size does not (H4/H5). Candidate explanation for a confirmatory test: the FP checkpoint sits at a point where a specific failure mode on these layouts is fragile to structured weight error (low-rank / Hessian-aligned), not that quantization adds a useful skill. X6 (half error) tells whether the effect needs the full error size.

## X6. Does half of the 2-bit error give half of the gain? (Q4; 8 Nov 07:40)
- **What:** `mxh50` = W + 0.5 (Wq - W) on all 432 quantized weights (the QuIP# error direction at half size). Same tasks and pairing as X5.
- **Why:** H4/H5 show isotropic noise of matched size does not give the gain. If half of the structured error gives a partial gain, the gain follows the direction of the 2-bit error, not its size; if it gives none, the gain needs the full error (a threshold).
- **Result (8 Nov 18:00):** mxh50 carrot 100, stack_cube 72 of 120 (FP 75 / 36): +25.4 [+16.2, +35.0], about the full gain (QuIP# +27.1). Gained-episode overlap with QuIP#: carrot 0.78, stack_cube 0.47. **Reading (exploratory):** half of the error gives the full gain, so there is no proportional dose response between 0.5 and 1; the effect saturates at or below half size. Together with X5: many different partial 2-bit errors give the same gain on the same episodes.

## X10-X12. Size, sign and the part along W of the QuIP# error (Q4; 8 Nov 18:10)
- **Input (CPU, explore/x10/quip_shrink.json):** per tensor, the QuIP# 0-sweeps error has median relative Frobenius size 0.40 (backbone 0.39, head 0.40); its projection on W is c = -0.052 (range -0.070 to -0.041, negative in all 432 tensors), but this part holds only 1.7% of the error energy; the norm of Wq is 2.8% above that of W. So the error shrinks every tensor a little along W and adds a large orthogonal part.
- **What (3 rows, `groot/mix.py`, same 2 tasks and pairing as X5):** `mxq25` = W + 0.25 (Wq - W); `mxneg` = W - (Wq - W) (sign flipped, same size); `mxpar` = W + c W per tensor (only the part along W: a uniform 5% shrink, no orthogonal part). 3 builds (7 GB each, freed by fin) and 6 evaluation jobs.
- **Why:** X6 says half the error is enough. mxq25 finds where the effect starts. mxneg tells direction against distance: if the flipped error also gives the gain, the gain comes from moving away from the FP point in this subspace (any sign), not from the specific 2-bit direction. mxpar tests the one systematic part of the error (shrink): if it alone gives the gain, the explanation is a simple weight-scale effect, which a confirmatory test can then pin down.
- **Protocol slip (8 Nov 18:58):** my count script read every `widowx_*.json` that existed, and the stack_cube files of mxneg and mxpar were still being written (jobs running). Those two partial counts are not used and not recorded; only tasks with a `.done` file are read. The script now checks the `.done` file first.
- **Result (8 Nov 19:40; all 6 tasks with `.done`; `s5bq/paired/x10_<row>.json`).** Successes of 120 (FP carrot 75, stack_cube 36; QuIP# 107, 69):

  | row | carrot | stack_cube | paired diff [95%] | gained-episode Jaccard with QuIP# (carrot / stack) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | mxq25 (0.25 of the error) | 89 | 75 | +22.1 [+12.9, +31.7] | 0.59 / 0.47 |
  | mxneg (error with sign flipped) | 100 | 19 | +3.3 [-7.5, +13.8] | 0.77 / 0.15 |
  | mxpar (only the part along W: shrink of 4 to 7%) | 102 | 67 | +24.2 [+14.2, +35.0] | 0.90 / 0.56 |

- **Reading (exploratory, no claim):** the small shrink part of the error (1.7% of its energy) gives almost the full gain on both tasks, on the same episodes as QuIP#. A quarter of the error (shrink about 1.3%, plus a quarter of the orthogonal part) already gives the full stack_cube gain. Flipping the sign (weights grow by about 5%, orthogonal part reversed) keeps the carrot gain but drops stack_cube below FP. Pre-registered next: H8 (uniform 0.95 W on 7 tasks; 1.05 W as a control), RIGOUR_2026-11-06.md.
- **CPU check (8 Nov 19:55; `explore/x10/{qtip,quip}_w2_libero10_shrink.json`):** on the GR00T LIBERO-10 checkpoints (the Bridge QTIP / VPTQ / AQLM checkpoints were freed), the QTIP 2-bit error also has a negative projection on W in all 432 tensors (median c = -0.058, range -0.072 to -0.038; 3.1% of the error energy), and QuIP# the same as on Bridge (c = -0.051). So the shrink is a property of the 2-bit quantizers here, not of one checkpoint. (LIBERO rows are below FP, so the shrink does not help on every benchmark.)

## X14. W2 (GOAL v8): where the shrink acts (8 Nov 20:50)
- **What:** 0.95 W on one group only, carrot + stack_cube, paired with FP: `mxsbb` backbone (216), `mxshd` action head (216), `mxsqk` attention q and k projections (to_q, to_k, q_proj, k_proj: 104 tensors; the 24 fused vision qkv are left out), `mxsvo` attention v and output (to_v, to_out, v_proj, o_proj, vision attn.proj: 128), `mxsmlp` MLPs (ff.net, mlp., mergers: 176). 5 builds and 10 evaluation jobs.
