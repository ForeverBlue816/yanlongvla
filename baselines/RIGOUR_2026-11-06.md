## C1. Numbers audit
- **Question:** is every success count, rate and paired difference in RESULTS.md and REPORT_2026-11-06.md what the raw per-unit files give?
- **Claim it can weaken:** every table in RESULTS.md.
- **Cost:** CPU only, seconds. 1000 units per Plus row (GR00T and pi0.5), 600 episodes per Fractal row.
- **Reading fixed in advance:** any count that differs from the per-unit file is a mismatch and the document is corrected; a difference or interval that differs by more than 0.2 pp from the recomputed paired value (same normal approximation) is also listed. Bits are checked in C3.
- Script: `jobs/experiments/rigour_c1_c2.py`; output `experiments/rigour_v5/`.

## C2. Which differences are real
- **Question:** which pairs of rows differ by more than noise?
- **Claim it can weaken:** every use of "better", "worse" or "best" in RESULTS.md, the report and EXPLANATIONS.md.
- **Cost:** CPU only. All pairs of rows with 1000 finished units (GR00T 18 rows incl. reruns, pi0.5 per row set), plus Fractal rows.
- **Reading fixed in advance:** paired difference with a 95% normal interval on the per-unit differences; p-value from the same statistic, Holm correction over all comparisons of one model. A pair is "real" only if it passes Holm at 0.05 AND its absolute net difference is larger than the largest net difference between two runs of the same configuration (the E0 floor: FP vs FP rerun, QTIP vs QTIP rerun). Otherwise the word "better" is not used for it.

## C5. Overlap of the solved sets
- **Question:** is QTIP a noisier copy of QuIP# (same units solved, different count) or does it solve different units?
- **Claim it can weaken:** the reading "QTIP and QuIP# are in the same class" (C2: not separated on GR00T) and any sentence that treats a count difference as a difference in the units solved.
- **Cost:** CPU, seconds. `jobs/experiments/rigour_c5_overlap.py` -> `experiments/rigour_v5/c5_overlap.md` (every pair of main rows, both models).
- **Reading fixed in advance:** the E0 floor flips 12 to 15% of the units under a tiny numerical change (agreement 85 to 88% for one and the same model). If agreement of two rows is within that band and the only-A / only-B counts are balanced, the rows solve the same units up to noise (copy). If agreement is clearly below it, the rows solve partly different units.
- **Result:** GR00T: QTIP and QuIP# 10 sweeps agree on 79.2% of the units (both 448, only QTIP 125, only QuIP# 83), QTIP and QuIP# 0 sweeps on 79.7% (111 / 92), QuIP# 10 and 0 sweeps on 81.1% (83 / 106). FP against QTIP: 81.1%. pi0.5: QTIP against QuIP# 0 sweeps 87.0% (58 / 72, inside the floor band), QTIP against QuIP# 10 sweeps 84.0%, FP against QTIP 86.3%.
- **Reading:** on pi0.5 QTIP and QuIP# 0 sweeps are inside the noise band, same units up to noise. On GR00T all main rows agree with each other on 75 to 81% of the units, 5 to 10 points below the E0 band: each row loses a different set of about 80 to 190 FP-solved units, and the rows gain 70 to 90 units that FP does not solve. So on GR00T the rows are not copies of each other; their differences in counts are the net of two larger, partly opposite movements of the units. This supports the C2 reading that count differences of 2 to 4 points between the top rows are not a ranking of how well the weights are kept.

## C3. Bits audit
- **Question:** are the linear bits per weight of RESULTS.md what the stored payload gives, counted independently of the quantizers' own accounting?
- **Claim it can weaken:** the "2.00 / 2.02 bits" comparability of the rows (every GR00T row table).
- **Cost:** CPU, minutes. `jobs/experiments/rigour_c3_bits.py` reads the safetensors headers of every layer file in `q/<row>/{backbone,head_*}` and sums the bytes of every tensor except the runtime `weight`; divides by the original (unpadded) weights of the same 432 layers. Rows with stored payload: QTIP, QuIP# (both sweeps use one payload layout), AQLM. VPTQ, GPTQ and AWQ payloads were deleted (their bits cannot be recomputed here, only read from their accounting).
- **Reading fixed in advance:** a recomputed value within 0.002 bpw of the table is confirmed; a larger difference is a mismatch to be explained by a named term (padding, tables, scales).
- **Result** (`experiments/rigour_v5/c3_bits.md`): stored payload equals `payload_bytes` of every layer json (432 of 432 for each row). QTIP 2.0175 (table 2.018, confirmed), AQLM 2.0195 (table 2.020, confirmed), QuIP# 2.0145 (table 2.031, **mismatch of 0.0165**). The QuIP# accounting adds the shared E8P12 lattice tables (2,420,744 bytes) once for the backbone group and once more for each head group, although the payload rule says "shared lattice tables once" and the tables are the same. Counted once per model the QuIP# linear bits are 2.0145 + 8 x 2,420,744 / 2,340.42 M = **2.0228**, not 2.031. Both values are above the 2.02 rule; 2.0228 is 0.0028 above it.
- **Effect on the text:** the QuIP# linear bpw in the GR00T table (2.031) is an over-count by 0.0083; the corrected value is 2.023. Whole-model bpw of the two QuIP# rows (5.601) is over-counted by the same bytes (about 0.002 per weight of the whole model); the table is not edited until the mentor decides whether the lattice tables count once per model (our reading) or once per checkpoint group (the accounting as run); footnote added to RESULTS.md. The ranking does not depend on it: QuIP# is still within 0.01 bpw of QTIP.

## C4. Task 6 b and c on the stored per-unit files (GR00T Plus 1000)
- **Question (b):** per suite and perturbation category (and difficulty level), where do the rows lose against FP, with clustered intervals? **(c):** units FP fails but a quantized row solves: are they a sign that quantization helps some units, or the noise of FP (E0)?
- **Claim it can weaken:** any sentence in the report or EXPLANATIONS.md that reads "the quantized row solves units FP fails" as a property of quantization.
- **Cost:** CPU, seconds. `jobs/experiments/rigour_c4_units.py` -> `experiments/rigour_v5/c4_units.md`; the control uses the mentor's FP file (`groot/data/reference/groot_fp.plus_1000.jsonl`), which differs from ours on 128 units by a numerical difference only (E0).
- **Reading fixed in advance:** if the gain rate (FP fails, row solves) is much higher on units that the mentor's FP solves (FP-marginal units) than on units both FPs fail, the gains are FP noise, not an effect of quantization; if equal, the gains are a property of the quantized model.
- **Result (b):** see `c4_units.md`. Losses are concentrated where FP is high; the large per-cell losses of AQLM and GPTQ best are visible in libero_10 difficulty 5 and libero_goal difficulty 3 to 5. Most cells for QTIP have intervals that include 0 (cells hold 20 to 85 units, so a cell needs a loss of 15 to 20 points to separate).
- **Result (c):** gains (FP fails, row solves) per row: QTIP 72, QuIP# 10 sweeps 81, QuIP# 0 sweeps 76, VPTQ 68, AQLM 49, GPTQ best 75, of 382 FP-failed units; losses 117, 168, 140, 191, 266, 217 of 618. **Control:** of the 382 FP-failed units, 64 are solved by the mentor's FP. Rows solve 48 to 53% of those 64 (AQLM 31%) against 9 to 16% of the 318 that both FPs fail. Nine units are solved by all five main rows (expected if independent: 0.1); the mentor's FP solves 5 of these 9.
- **Reading:** per the pre-registered rule, the gains are mostly FP-marginal units (flipping under any numerical change), not quantization helping. The 9 to 16% that remain on units both FPs fail are the same order as the E0 flip rate and are not read as an effect. This is E8 in EXPLANATIONS.md. Videos of failing units were not stored for LIBERO, so no claim about how the policy fails is made from videos.

## R6. Decode check (partial, CPU, AQLM only)
- **Question:** does the stored payload decode to the weight that was evaluated?
- **Claim it can weaken:** that the evaluated fake-quant weights equal what the stored codes represent (and so that the bits counted in C3 buy the evaluated model).
- **Cost:** CPU, seconds; two layers of the AQLM backbone (`layers.0.mlp.down_proj`, `layers.15.self_attn.q_proj`), decoded with a six-line independent decoder (scale x sum over the two codebooks of codebook[code]) in float32 and compared with the stored `weight` (bf16).
- **Reading fixed in advance:** relative difference near the bf16 rounding scale (about 1.6e-3) -> the payload decodes to the evaluated weight; anything larger -> a mismatch to chase.
- **Result:** relative difference 1.66e-3 on both layers (max abs 9.7e-4 on weights up to 0.35): bf16 rounding of the stored weight, nothing else. So the AQLM payload decodes to the evaluated weight on these two layers. Not done: QTIP (trellis decoder) and QuIP# (E8P12 decoder) need the toolkit decoders on a GPU, and VPTQ/GPTQ/AWQ payloads are not stored; these stay open and are not claimed.
