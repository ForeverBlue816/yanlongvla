What the numbers say:

- **"2-bit above FP" is reproduced here, with our own code.** Our clean row is +8.9 pp above our FP and the QTIP row rebuilt here +9.2 pp,
  both intervals excluding 0, from the same tasks as in Chamadol's table (carrot_on_plate, stack_cube; here also put_eggplant_in_basket).
  So his finding is not an evaluation error. His explanation (H8: every 2-bit error shrinks each weight along itself by 4 to 7 %, and a
  plain 0.95 × W gives the same gain) is consistent with our rows: all 432 layers of all four rows have a negative projection (last line
  of the first table; `results/lambda_1bit_20261112/bridge_weight_shrink.json`, `results/lambda_1bit_20261112/code/bridge_weight_shrink.py`).
- **Neither step of our method helps on Bridge.** H_D lowers the clean row by 4.4 pp (interval touches 0), the fine-tune lowers the
  base by another 3.7 pp (interval excludes 0). The held-out action error orders the three rows the same way (0.116 < 0.140 < 0.152).
  This is the opposite of LIBERO-Plus, where the same recipe gives the best 2-bit row.
- **The fine-tune hardly moved its own objective**: held-out L_readout 10,347 at update 0, 10,196 at the exported update 384 (−1.5 %),
  10,406 at update 768; 22 non-finite steps skipped (limit 38); 17.1 h. On the 1-bit LIBERO rows the same objective fell by 14 to 46 %.
- **The lower result of the main row is not a loss of the shrink**: the fine-tuned row has the same weight shrink as its base (−0.061).
  Why the fine-tune costs 3.7 pp is not identified.
- **Per-episode agreement with FP is the same for all rows** (661 to 678 of 840), so the main row is not "closer to FP" episode by
  episode, although its mean equals FP's.
- **One difference to Chamadol's QTIP row is large**: put_eggplant_in_basket is 86 here and 27 in his table (spoon 110 against 100; the
  other tasks within 2). The row here is his scripts (`baselines/groot/`, `GROOT_PROFILE=bridge`: `prepare_fractal.py --name bridge`,
  `calibrate.py`, `export_layers.py`, `qtip/quantize_qtip.py --bits 2`, `import_external.py`) on H100; QTIP's two CUDA extensions
  (`fast_hadamard_transform`, `qtip_kernels`) could not be built here and are replaced by a pure-PyTorch Hadamard transform and an empty
  module (`results/lambda_1bit_20261112/code/run_with_stubs.py`, chain `code/qtip_chain.sh`; the kernels are not called while quantizing). His table notes that this task flips
  24 of 120 episodes between two identical FP runs, but 59 episodes is more than that; the cause is open.

Caveats: (1) the on-policy source of H_D was captured in SimplerEnv on the same finite set of layouts that the evaluation runs (other
noise seeds; one policy input per rollout; user decision 2026-11-11) — an advantage for our rows, which did not turn into a gain;
(2) one evaluation seed and one fit per row; (3) an FP repeat with the same seed is running on GPU 0 (`eval/fp_repeat`) to give the
per-episode noise floor on H100 (Chamadol's GPU: 809 of 840 equal).

Main-row pipeline (`scripts/groot_simpler_main.py`): clean-H 2-bit row `tcq_w2_rht` (432 layers, 590,290,944 B, 2.0177 / 5.5915 bpw) →
256 on-policy states in SimplerEnv (176 successful rollouts) → H_D (mean source weights 0.333–0.358) → D1 fit (432 layers, damping 0.01
on all) → V0' fine-tune (one checkpoint, so no suite routing; hold-out 40 / 256; L_vel window = 4 executed of 8). Per-episode files:
`results/lambda_1bit_20261112/bridge_<row>.episodes_{a,b}.json`; paired statistics `bridge_paired_<row>_vs_<reference>.json`.
